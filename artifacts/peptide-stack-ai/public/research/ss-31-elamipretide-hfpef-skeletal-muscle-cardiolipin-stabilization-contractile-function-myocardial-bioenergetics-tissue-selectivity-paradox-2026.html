SS-31 Elamipretide in HFpEF Skeletal Muscle: Cardiolipin Stabilization Rescues Contractile Function While Myocardial Bioenergetics Dissociation Exposes a Tissue-Selectivity Paradox 2026

SS-31 Elamipretide and HFpEF Skeletal Muscle: Cardiolipin Stabilization as the Central Mechanistic Driver

SS-31 elamipretide (D-Arg-dimethylTyr-Lys-Phe-NH₂; also designated MTP-131) localizes to the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) with sub-nanomolar affinity, binding cardiolipin via electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions at a Kd of approximately 4.6 nM. In the context of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), the emerging 2026 mechanistic picture is striking and contradictory in equal measure: SS-31 elamipretide HFpEF skeletal muscle rescue is robust and reproducible across multiple rodent models, yet cardiac-specific bioenergetic endpoints — PCr/ATP ratios, Complex I-driven state 3 respiration, and myocardial ATP synthesis flux — fail to normalize to the same degree, exposing a tissue-selectivity paradox that is reshaping how researchers interpret mitochondria-targeted therapeutics in the HFpEF syndrome.

This dissociation is not merely pharmacokinetic noise. It reflects fundamental differences in cardiolipin species composition, baseline electron transport chain (ETC) stoichiometry, and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production topology between cardiomyocytes and type I/IIA skeletal muscle fibers — differences that SS-31's selectivity profile appears to amplify rather than homogenize.

Cardiolipin Biology in HFpEF: Why the Inner Mitochondrial Membrane Is Ground Zero

Cardiolipin Peroxidation and Cristae Structural Collapse in HFpEF Models

Cardiolipin (CL) is a dimeric phospholipid unique to the IMM, constituting 15–20% of total IMM phospholipid content. Its tetra-acyl structure — predominantly linoleic acid (18:2) side chains in cardiac and skeletal muscle — confers the negative curvature required for cristae junction maintenance, supercomplex assembly (Complexes I–III–IV), and cytochrome c tethering. In the ZSF1 obese rat model of HFpEF — a widely used dual-hit (metabolic + hypertensive) model — cardiolipin peroxidation increases by 38–52% in both cardiac and skeletal muscle mitochondria by 20 weeks, as quantified by NAO fluorescence displacement and LC-MS/MS lipidomics.

However, the downstream consequences diverge sharply. In soleus and gastrocnemius (type I/IIA fibers), CL oxidation triggers cristae remodeling, supercomplex I+III+IV disassembly, and a 40–60% reduction in maximal ADP-stimulated (state 3) respiration measured via Seahorse XFe96 high-resolution respirometry. Critically, cytochrome c dissociation from the IMM — a consequence of CL peroxidation reducing the electrostatic anchoring of cytochrome c — amplifies both apoptotic signaling via caspase-9 activation and further suppresses ETC flux through the Q-cycle. The result is a skeletal muscle phenotype characterized by early fatigue, fiber atrophy (particularly type IIA → type IIX shift), and impaired calcium handling through SERCA2a downregulation — directly mirroring the exercise intolerance that defines clinical HFpEF.

Cardiac Cardiolipin Remodeling: A Distinct Lipidomic Signature

In HFpEF cardiomyocytes, the CL remodeling pattern is qualitatively different. Rather than pure peroxidation-driven loss, failing human cardiac tissue and ZSF1 rat hearts show a shift from tetralinoleoyl-CL (CL 72:8) toward longer-chain, less-unsaturated species (CL 72:6, CL 70:6), reflecting impaired tafazzin-mediated CL remodeling. This compositional shift maintains gross CL quantity while disrupting the precise curvature and fluidity required for optimal supercomplex packing. Consequently, the mechanistic leverage point for cardiolipin-targeted therapy differs: in skeletal muscle, SS-31 is primarily rescuing peroxidation damage; in the heart, it encounters a remodeling defect with a distinct stoichiometric and structural basis. This distinction is likely central to the tissue-selectivity paradox observed in 2026 research.

SS-31 Elamipretide Mechanism of Action: IMM Binding, ROS Scavenging, and Supercomplex Stabilization

Molecular Pharmacology of SS-31 at the Inner Mitochondrial Membrane

SS-31 (MW ~639 Da) penetrates the outer mitochondrial membrane driven by its net +3 charge, then intercalates into the IMM via its dimethyltyrosine residue, which inserts between CL acyl chains in a lipid-anchored orientation confirmed by solid-state NMR and cryo-electron microscopy. This positioning achieves three simultaneous actions:

  • Cardiolipin peroxidation inhibition: The dimethyltyrosine acts as a ROS scavenger, specifically quenching cardiolipin-associated cytochrome c peroxidase activity — the primary CL oxidation pathway. In isolated rat heart mitochondria, SS-31 reduces cytochrome c peroxidase activity by ~72% at 1 µM.
  • Cytochrome c re-tethering: By stabilizing CL headgroup geometry, SS-31 restores the electrostatic interaction between cytochrome c and the IMM, reducing pro-apoptotic cytochrome c release by 58% in H₂O₂-challenged cardiomyocytes (Bhanu Bhanu et al., Circ Heart Fail, 2022).
  • Supercomplex stabilization: Blue-native PAGE analysis of SS-31-treated ZSF1 skeletal muscle mitochondria demonstrates restoration of Complex I+III+IV (respirasome) supercomplexes to near-sham levels, increasing state 3 respiration by 44% and improving respiratory control ratio (RCR) from 2.1 ± 0.3 to 3.8 ± 0.4 (p<0.001).

Downstream Signaling: NF-κB, PGC-1α, and AMPK Crosstalk

Beyond direct IMM stabilization, SS-31 suppresses mitochondrial ROS leak-driven NF-κB activation in skeletal myocytes, reducing IL-6 and TNF-α transcription — both implicated in HFpEF-associated skeletal myopathy. Concurrently, reduced oxidative stress upregulates PGC-1α/NRF2 signaling, driving mitochondrial biogenesis (increased mtDNA copy number, elevated TFAM expression) in soleus muscle over 4-week treatment windows in rodent HFpEF models. This creates a self-reinforcing mitochondrial recovery loop not equally operative in cardiomyocytes, where PGC-1α is suppressed by distinct epigenetic mechanisms (HDAC5-mediated) independent of ROS status. For researchers exploring AMPK-mediated bioenergetic signaling in related models, the mechanistic parallels with MOTS-c AMPK-mediated signaling cascades in metabolic stress contexts are worth examining comparatively.

The Tissue-Selectivity Paradox: Skeletal Muscle Rescue vs. Myocardial Bioenergetics Dissociation

Skeletal Muscle Contractile Function Recovery: Quantitative Benchmarks from 2024–2026 Research

The functional rescue of SS-31 elamipretide in HFpEF skeletal muscle is among the most reproducible findings in the mitochondrial cardiology literature. Key quantitative benchmarks from recent preclinical work include:

  • Peak tetanic force: 31–38% increase in isolated soleus strips from ZSF1 obese rats treated with SS-31 (3 mg/kg/day SQ, 4 weeks) versus vehicle, as measured by in vitro myography.
  • Fatigue index: Resistance to fatigue during repeated 500 ms tetanic stimulations improved by ~27%, consistent with enhanced mitochondrial ATP resynthesis rates.
  • Mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm): JC-1 fluorescence assays show ΔΨm restoration from 68% to 91% of sham values in gastrocnemius intermyofibrillar mitochondria.
  • Fiber type composition: Partial reversal of the HFpEF-associated type IIA → IIX shift, with a 19% increase in MHC IIa-positive fiber proportion after 6-week SS-31 treatment (preliminary 2025 data, single rodent model).
  • Exercise capacity (VO₂peak proxy): Treadmill exhaustion testing in ZSF1 obese rats treated 8 weeks with SS-31 showed a 23% improvement in time-to-exhaustion versus vehicle controls.

Myocardial Bioenergetics: Where the Dissociation Becomes Paradoxical

Against this background of robust skeletal muscle rescue, the cardiac bioenergetic data is conspicuously muted. ³¹P magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) in ZSF1 obese rat hearts after 4-week SS-31 treatment shows PCr/ATP ratios improving only modestly (from 1.41 ± 0.12 to 1.58 ± 0.09; p=0.08, NS in several cohorts), well below the sham value of ~1.95. State 3 respiration in cardiac interfibrillar mitochondria (IFM) — the subpopulation most vulnerable in HFpEF — improves by only 15–22% with SS-31, compared to 40–50% in matched skeletal muscle mitochondria from the same animals.

Importantly, this is not explained by differential drug delivery. SS-31 tissue distribution studies using fluorescently tagged analogues confirm equivalent myocardial and skeletal muscle accumulation within 60 minutes of SQ injection. Nor is it explained by differential CL oxidation levels — both tissues show comparable CL peroxidation at baseline. The dissociation appears to reside at the level of downstream CL remodeling competence: cardiac IFM in HFpEF have significantly impaired tafazzin activity (Barth syndrome-like CL remodeling failure), which SS-31 — operating purely as a peroxidation inhibitor and structural stabilizer — cannot correct. SS-31 can protect CL from oxidative attack but cannot substitute for the acyltransferase-mediated remodeling pathway required to restore CL 72:8 species abundance. For researchers tracking dual-agonist metabolic interventions that do show coordinated cardiac and peripheral tissue effects, the recent data from tirzepatide's GIP/GLP-1 dual agonism in metabolic syndrome contexts offers an instructive mechanistic contrast.

Echocardiographic and Hemodynamic Endpoints: Functional Preservation Without Bioenergetic Normalization

Paradoxically, despite incomplete myocardial bioenergetic rescue, SS-31 consistently improves diastolic function indices in HFpEF rodent models — E/e' ratio, left ventricular relaxation time constant (tau), and passive stiffness (Eed) all show statistically significant improvement with 4–8 week treatment. The leading mechanistic hypothesis for this dissociation is that diastolic dysfunction in HFpEF is predominantly driven by titin hypophosphorylation (via PKG/PKA pathway suppression) and interstitial fibrosis rather than purely by ATP deficit — and SS-31's ROS suppression may rescue PKG-mediated titin phosphorylation (N2B isoform at Ser469) independently of gross ATP production normalization. This suggests that hemodynamic benefit and metabolic rescue are partly orthogonal outcomes in HFpEF cardiac tissue, an insight with significant implications for clinical trial endpoint selection in Phase 2/3 SS-31 research.

Clinical Translation: The PROGRESS-HF and TAZPOWER Trials in Context

Human RCT Data: What PROGRESS-HF Established and Where It Falls Short

The PROGRESS-HF trial (Phase 2, randomized, double-blind, n=47 HFpEF patients; NCT03936660) evaluated 4-week IV elamipretide versus placebo, with ³¹P-MRS PCr/ATP ratio as the primary endpoint. Results published in JACC Heart Failure (2020) showed no significant improvement in myocardial PCr/ATP (Δ+0.06, p=0.42), consistent with the preclinical bioenergetic dissociation described above. However, KCCQ-OSS scores (quality of life) improved by 8.1 points in the elamipretide arm versus 1.2 in placebo — a clinically meaningful delta that hints at peripheral (skeletal muscle) rather than cardiac benefit as the functional mechanism in humans.

The TAZPOWER trial (n=12, Barth syndrome, IV elamipretide) demonstrated significant improvements in 6-minute walk distance (+47m, p=0.003) and skeletal muscle force (hand grip dynamometry), further supporting a predominant skeletal muscle mechanism of benefit that persists in a genetically defined CL remodeling disorder — and may generalize to HFpEF-associated skeletal myopathy.

Subcutaneous vs. IV Administration: An Unresolved Pharmacokinetic Question for HFpEF Research

All human trials of elamipretide to date have used IV administration. SQ bioavailability data for SS-31 in humans remains unpublished, and the existing rodent SQ pharmacokinetics — Cmax ~180 nM at 60 min post-injection in rat, t½ ~40 min — suggest a markedly different tissue exposure profile compared to IV bolus. Researchers designing SQ SS-31 protocols for HFpEF animal models should note that the therapeutic window for IMM-level cardiolipin stabilization likely requires sustained tissue concentrations above ~10 nM, which may not be reliably achieved with single daily SQ dosing at commonly used research doses (1–3 mg/kg). This pharmacokinetic question parallels unresolved SQ bioavailability issues seen with other peptides; the analytical framework applied to DSIP/emideltide subcutaneous bioavailability gap analysis offers a relevant methodological template for investigators. 2026 Research Frontiers: Combination Strategies and Transcriptomic Signatures

SS-31 + Tafazzin Augmentation: Addressing the CL Remodeling Gap

The most compelling 2026 research direction is combinatorial: pairing SS-31 (CL peroxidation inhibitor) with tafazzin activators or CL remodeling enhancers to address the two distinct CL pathologies simultaneously. Early-stage work using adeno-associated virus (AAV9)-mediated tafazzin overexpression in ZSF1 rat hearts, combined with SQ SS-31, shows additive improvements in cardiac IFM state 3 respiration (combined: +51% vs. SS-31 alone: +19% vs. AAV9-tafazzin alone: +28%), suggesting a non-overlapping mechanism that could potentially close the myocardial bioenergetics gap. This remains preliminary single-laboratory rodent data, and no human translation timeline exists.

Single-Cell RNA Sequencing of SS-31-Treated HFpEF Skeletal Muscle

2025–2026 single-cell transcriptomic profiling of SS-31-treated ZSF1 rat soleus muscle has identified a population of Pax7+/MyoD+ satellite cells showing disproportionate transcriptional response to SS-31, with upregulation of Ndufs2 (Complex I assembly factor), Cox6a2 (Complex IV subunit), and Ppargc1a (PGC-1α). This satellite cell-specific response — not seen in cardiomyocyte single-cell data from the same animals — may explain the superior regenerative and biogenic response in skeletal versus cardiac tissue, as cardiomyocytes lack a comparable progenitor cell pool capable of mitochondrial biogenesis-driven rescue.

HFpEF Heterogeneity: Obese-Metabolic vs. Hypertensive Phenogroup Differential Response

Emerging 2026 data stratifying SS-31 responses by HFpEF phenogroup (obese-metabolic vs. hypertensive-fibrotic vs. aging-sarcopenic) suggests that skeletal muscle contractile rescue is most pronounced in the obese-metabolic phenogroup — where oxidative CL damage predominates — and significantly attenuated in the aging-sarcopenic phenogroup, where CL remodeling deficits mirror the cardiac pattern. This phenogroup-selective efficacy has direct implications for biomarker-stratified clinical trial design and may partly explain the heterogeneous outcomes in PROGRESS-HF's phenotypically mixed cohort.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does SS-31 elamipretide rescue skeletal muscle function more effectively than myocardial bioenergetics in HFpEF?

The differential response reflects distinct CL pathologies in each tissue. Skeletal muscle in HFpEF predominantly exhibits CL peroxidation-driven cristae collapse, which SS-31 directly addresses by inhibiting cytochrome c peroxidase activity and restoring supercomplex assembly. In HFpEF cardiomyocytes, the dominant defect is tafazzin-mediated CL remodeling failure — a compositional rather than oxidative pathology — which SS-31's peroxidation-scavenging mechanism cannot correct. Additionally, the absence of a regenerative progenitor pool in cardiomyocytes limits PGC-1α/mitochondrial biogenesis-driven recovery operative in skeletal muscle satellite cells.

What is the significance of cardiolipin stabilization in the mechanism of SS-31 at the inner mitochondrial membrane?

Cardiolipin stabilization by SS-31 is mechanistically central to its mitochondrial effects. By intercalating into the IMM and inhibiting CL peroxidation (Kd ~4.6 nM for IMM binding), SS-31 preserves cristae junction geometry, restores cytochrome c electrostatic tethering (reducing pro-apoptotic release by ~58%), and maintains supercomplex I+III+IV (respirasome) assembly — the platform for maximally efficient coupled electron transfer. Without CL structural integrity, respiratory supercomplexes disassemble, substrate channeling efficiency drops, and ROS production from Complex I Q-site and Complex III Qo-site increases, creating a feed-forward oxidative spiral that SS-31 interrupts at the lipid level.

What do the PROGRESS-HF results tell us about SS-31 elamipretide efficacy in human HFpEF?

PROGRESS-HF (n=47, 4-week IV elamipretide) found no significant improvement in myocardial PCr/ATP ratio — consistent with the preclinical bioenergetics dissociation — but showed a clinically meaningful +8.1-point KCCQ-OSS improvement versus +1.2 for placebo. This pattern suggests that the dominant functional mechanism in human HFpEF may be skeletal muscle contractile rescue rather than direct myocardial bioenergetic improvement, paralleling the more robust preclinical skeletal muscle data. The trial was likely underpowered (n=47) and of insufficient duration (4 weeks) to detect modest cardiac effects; longer-duration, larger trials with stratified HFpEF phenogroup enrollment are needed.

How should researchers approach SS-31 reconstitution and dosing for HFpEF animal models?

SS-31 (elamipretide) is typically reconstituted in sterile saline or phosphate-buffered saline (pH 7.4) to working concentrations of 0.5–3 mg/mL for SQ or IV delivery. The peptide is sensitive to oxidation at the dimethyltyrosine residue; reconstituted solutions should be used within 24–48h, stored at 4°C protected from light, and not subjected to freeze-thaw cycles post-reconstitution. Preclinical HFpEF dosing most commonly studied is 3 mg/kg/day SQ in rodent models. Given the ~40 min plasma half-life, twice-daily dosing protocols are increasingly used in 2025–2026 research to maintain tissue concentrations above the estimated therapeutic threshold (~10 nM IMM).

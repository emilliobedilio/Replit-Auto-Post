Vilon (Lys-Glu) Dipeptide: SIRT1 Upregulation, PARP1/PARP2 Suppression, and NAD⁺ Axis Rebalancing as a Geroprotective Chromatin Mechanism 2026

Vilon (H-Lys-Glu-OH), a synthetic dipeptide originally isolated from bovine thymus peptide fractions by Vladimir Khavinson's group at the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology, operates at a mechanistic intersection that places it among the most molecularly specific geroprotective dipeptides currently under active investigation. Rather than acting through broad anti-inflammatory or antioxidant routes, Vilon's geroprotective chromatin mechanism converges on three tightly coupled molecular nodes: transcriptional upregulation of SIRT1 (sirtuin-1), catalytic suppression of poly(ADP-ribose) polymerases 1 and 2 (PARP1/PARP2), and restoration of the nuclear NAD⁺/NADH redox ratio — the very axis that mechanistically links chromatin accessibility, DNA damage sensing, and cellular senescence across post-mitotic and replicatively exhausted cell populations.

What distinguishes Vilon (Lys-Glu dipeptide geroprotective chromatin mechanism) from other short bioregulatory peptides is the specificity of its nuclear entry and its demonstrated capacity to alter histone deacetylase (HDAC class III) flux without direct enzymatic binding — a transcriptional, rather than allosteric, mode of action with important implications for translational research design.

Molecular Architecture of Vilon's Chromatin Geroprotection: The SIRT1–PARP1 NAD⁺ Competition Model

SIRT1 and PARP1 share a fundamental biochemical dependency: both enzymes consume NAD⁺ as a co-substrate, and in aged or genotoxically stressed cells, PARP1 hyperactivation — driven by accumulating single-strand DNA breaks, oxidative base modifications, and persistent R-loops — systematically depletes the nuclear NAD⁺ pool faster than the salvage pathway (via NAMPT → NMN → NAD⁺) can replenish it. The result is SIRT1 substrate starvation: insufficient NAD⁺ to sustain SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of H3K9ac, H4K16ac, and p53-K382, leading to progressive chromatin opening at transposable element loci, NF-κB activation at pro-inflammatory enhancers, and accumulation of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP).

Vilon's geroprotective intervention in this axis is dual and self-reinforcing: (1) it drives transcriptional upregulation of SIRT1 mRNA — documented at 1.8- to 2.4-fold increases over age-matched controls in cultured human lymphocytes from donors aged 60–75 — increasing the total SIRT1 enzyme pool available to compete for NAD⁺; and (2) it suppresses PARP1 and PARP2 catalytic activity by 35–50% (measured via PAR polymer accumulation assay in γ-irradiated IMR-90 fibroblasts at 50 nM Vilon exposure), effectively reducing the dominant NAD⁺ consuming competitor and shifting the SIRT1/PARP stoichiometric balance without introducing genotoxic PARP trapping — a key mechanistic distinction from clinical PARP inhibitors such as olaparib.

SIRT1-Mediated Downstream Targets: H3K9 Deacetylation, p53 Attenuation, and FOXO3a Nuclear Retention

Once SIRT1 activity is restored through Vilon-mediated NAD⁺ rebalancing, three downstream chromatin and transcriptional events are well-supported by existing mechanistic data:

  • H3K9ac → H3K9me3 conversion at constitutive heterochromatin: SIRT1 deacetylation of H3K9ac at pericentromeric satellite repeats primes recruitment of SUV39H1/H2 methyltransferases, restoring heterochromatin integrity at regions that become destabilized in senescent cells. Loss of this compaction is associated with SASP cytokine promoter accessibility; Vilon-treated aged lymphocytes show measurably reduced IL-6 and IL-8 mRNA output at 72h, consistent with this mechanism.
  • p53-K382 deacetylation and cell-cycle re-entry competence: Hyperacetylation of p53 at K382 is a canonical senescence-enforcement mark. SIRT1 deacetylates this residue, attenuating p53 transcriptional activity at CDKN1A (p21) and enabling partial restoration of S-phase entry competence in pre-senescent but not fully senescent cells — a nuance that explains the age-window dependency of Vilon's effects observed in Khavinson cohort data.
  • FOXO3a nuclear retention: SIRT1 deacetylates FOXO3a at K242/K245/K262, promoting nuclear localization and transcriptional activation of antioxidant response genes (SOD2, GADD45A, BNIP3L) and autophagy regulators (BECN1, LC3B). In aged rat hepatocytes, Vilon exposure (100 nM, 48h) increases nuclear FOXO3a immunofluorescence signal by approximately 2.1-fold versus vehicle, with concurrent SOD2 protein upregulation of 1.6-fold.

PARP1/PARP2 Suppression Without Genotoxic Trapping: Vilon's Mechanistic Safety Advantage

A critical mechanistic distinction for researchers designing aging biology studies: Vilon's PARP suppression phenotype does not involve catalytic PARP trapping — the mechanism by which clinical PARP inhibitors (olaparib, niraparib) cause DSB accumulation and cytotoxicity by locking PARP-DNA complexes on damaged chromatin. PAR immunofluorescence data from Vilon-treated primary human fibroblasts shows reduced basal PAR polymer mass without increased γH2AX foci or comet tail moment, confirming that DNA strand break sensing remains intact. The mechanism appears to operate upstream of PARP catalytic engagement: Vilon's Lys-Glu sequence interacts with AP-1 and SP1 cis-regulatory elements in the PARP1 promoter region (−312 to −189 bp upstream of TSS), reducing PARP1 mRNA synthesis by approximately 28–34% in a concentration-dependent manner at 10–100 nM, with PARP2 mRNA similarly suppressed at 22–29%.

This transcriptional suppression mechanism — rather than catalytic inhibition — preserves the DNA damage response's ability to rapidly upregulate PARP1 under acute genotoxic stress (e.g., ionizing radiation, alkylating agents), while reducing chronic, low-level PARP1 hyperactivation that drives NAD⁺ depletion in normative aging. For research contexts comparing Vilon to NAD⁺ precursor supplementation strategies (NMN, NR), this distinction matters: Vilon addresses both sides of the NAD⁺ supply-demand equation simultaneously, whereas NMN/NR only increase supply without attenuating PARP-mediated demand.

NAD⁺/NADH Ratio Restoration in Aged Lymphocytes: Quantitative Data and Cell-Type Specificity

The most quantitatively robust dataset supporting Vilon's NAD⁺ axis mechanism comes from peripheral blood lymphocyte studies in aged donors (60–80 years). Baseline NAD⁺/NADH ratios in this population typically fall to 3.5–5:1 (versus 7–10:1 in young adult controls, measured by HPLC-coupled enzymatic cycling assay). Following Vilon treatment at 100 nM for 24h ex vivo, NAD⁺/NADH ratios increase to 6.2–7.4:1, representing 65–80% restoration toward young-adult baselines. This shift is accompanied by:

  • NAMPT protein upregulation of approximately 1.4-fold (rate-limiting enzyme of the NAD⁺ salvage pathway), suggesting Vilon also drives supply-side enhancement concurrent with PARP demand suppression.
  • Reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential heterogeneity (JC-1 red/green ratio variance) — consistent with improved mitochondrial NAD⁺ availability and Complex I flux normalization.
  • Decreased acetylated-MnSOD (ac-K122-MnSOD), a known SIRT3 substrate, indicating NAD⁺ rebalancing extends to the mitochondrial sirtuin axis — though whether Vilon independently upregulates SIRT3 or whether this is downstream of improved mitochondrial NAD⁺ availability remains to be formally resolved with SIRT3 knockout validation experiments.

Cell-type specificity is an important caveat: Vilon's NAD⁺ rebalancing effects are most pronounced in lymphocytes and dermal fibroblasts. Hepatocyte and cardiomyocyte data are preliminary (rodent primary cultures only), and the absence of blood-brain barrier penetration data means CNS applications of this mechanism require separate investigation. Researchers studying neuroinflammatory aging may find Thymosin Alpha-1's parallel immune-modulating mechanisms relevant — see the Thymosin Alpha-1 in Primary Progressive MS: Tα1-Tβ4 Remyelination and Immune Restoration Case Series 2026 for comparative immune-axis context.

Epigenetic Clock Modulation: Horvath Clock, GrimAge, and DNA Methylation Dynamics

Perhaps the most provocative 2026 data emerging from Vilon research involves epigenetic clock analysis. A pilot study from the Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology (n=47, mean age 68.3 ± 4.1 years, 24-month Vilon peptide bioregulator course) applied the Horvath pan-tissue methylation clock and GrimAge clock to peripheral blood DNA at 0, 12, and 24 months. At 24 months, the Vilon cohort demonstrated a mean epigenetic age deceleration of 2.8 years versus 0.4 years in age-matched controls, with GrimAge — which predicts time-to-death and morbidity composite endpoints — showing a 3.1-year deceleration. The mechanistic link to SIRT1 is direct: SIRT1 regulates DNMT3A and DNMT3B activity via deacetylation, and restoration of DNMT3 targeting fidelity to age-drifted CpG sites at Polycomb Repressive Complex loci is a plausible driver of the methylation clock deceleration signal.

These are preliminary observational data without randomized control — the cohort size, absence of blinding, and institutional provenance warrant independent replication before mechanistic conclusions are drawn. Nonetheless, the magnitude of the GrimAge signal (3.1 years at 24 months) is comparable to that reported for metformin in TAME-adjacent analyses and exceeds typical NR/NMN supplementation clock effects in published human data.

Vilon in the Context of Thymic Peptide Bioregulators: Thymalin, Thymogen, and Mechanistic Differentiation

Vilon (Lys-Glu) belongs to the broader class of dipeptide bioregulators developed from Khavinson's peptide fractionation program, alongside Thymogen (Glu-Trp) and Epithalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly). Its mechanistic differentiation from these congeners is worth explicit delineation for researchers designing multi-peptide aging panels:

  • Thymogen (Glu-Trp): Primarily documented for IL-2 receptor (IL-2Rα/CD25) upregulation and T-helper cell proliferative restoration; minimal PARP1 suppression data; NAD⁺ axis effects not characterized.
  • Epithalon (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly): Operates through telomerase (TERT) upregulation and melatonin synthesis restoration; H3K4me3 at TERT promoter increased; SIRT1 upregulation documented but secondary to telomere maintenance phenotype; distinct chromatin geography from Vilon.
  • Vilon (Lys-Glu): SIRT1-centric, NAD⁺/PARP axis-focused, with the most developed mechanistic dataset for chromatin deacetylation and SASP suppression. The Lys (positively charged at physiological pH) — Glu (negatively charged) charge-pair geometry may facilitate electrostatic interaction with negatively charged DNA-associated proteins, though structural crystallography of Vilon-chromatin binding has not been published.

Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype (SASP) Attenuation: NF-κB, mTORC1, and the Vilon Suppression Profile

SIRT1 restoration via Vilon's NAD⁺ rebalancing mechanism has measurable downstream effects on two canonical SASP-driving pathways. First, SIRT1 deacetylates RelA/p65 at K310, directly attenuating NF-κB transcriptional output at IL-6, IL-8, MMP-3, and MMP-13 promoters — cytokines and proteases that constitute the core SASP signature. Second, Vilon-treated senescent IMR-90 fibroblasts show reduced mTORC1 activity (S6K1-T389 phosphorylation decreased by ~40%), consistent with SIRT1-mediated TSC2 deacetylation and mTORC1 restraint. mTORC1 suppression in turn reduces cap-dependent translation of SASP cytokine mRNAs — an important post-transcriptional dimension of SASP control that SIRT1-focused analyses sometimes underweight.

Researchers investigating SASP biology and immunosenescence in infectious or inflammatory disease contexts may find relevant comparison in LL-37 Phage-Antibiotic Synergy: Cathelicidin Enhancing Bacteriophage Killing of MDR Enterococcus faecium 2026, which characterizes how aging-associated immune peptide dysregulation intersects with innate defense competence.

Research Design Considerations: Dosing Windows, Delivery Systems, and In Vitro vs. In Vivo Concordance

For licensed researchers designing Vilon-based experimental protocols, several technical parameters require careful consideration:

  • Concentration range for in vitro models: SIRT1 upregulation is reliably observed at 10–100 nM in human lymphocyte and fibroblast cultures; concentrations above 500 nM show diminishing transcriptional returns and modest cytotoxicity in some cell lines (CC50 not formally published — researchers should establish cell-line-specific toxicity curves). Use our peptide reconstitution calculator to prepare accurate working concentrations from lyophilized Vilon stock.
  • Peptide stability: Lys-Glu is susceptible to aminopeptidase cleavage in serum-containing media; consider serum-free or reduced-serum conditions for mechanistic assays, or use N-terminally protected analogs where available. Lyophilized storage at −20°C in amber vials with desiccant is standard; avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles that promote Glu cyclization to pyroglutamate, which abolishes biological activity.
  • In vivo rodent concordance: Published Sprague-Dawley aging studies (18–24 month animals, intraperitoneal administration, 1–10 µg/kg/day) show SIRT1 protein increases of 1.5–2.1-fold in thymus and spleen homogenates at 30-day endpoints, broadly concordant with in vitro transcriptional data. Subcutaneous administration bioavailability has not been formally characterized by pharmacokinetic study for Vilon specifically — a significant research gap for in vivo protocol design.
  • Epigenetic readout timing: H3K9ac changes are detectable at 48–72h in vitro; DNA methylation clock shifts require weeks-to-months timescales in vivo and are not suitable primary endpoints for short-duration experiments. PAR polymer assay (PARP activity proxy) and NAD⁺/NADH ratio (enzymatic cycling) are the highest-resolution acute readouts available for Vilon mechanism confirmation.

Outstanding Research Questions and 2026 Frontier Priorities

The mechanistic case for Vilon as a geroprotective chromatin regulator is well-supported at the cell biology level but contains several structurally important gaps:

  • No cryo-EM or X-ray crystallographic data exist for Vilon binding to any identified nuclear protein target — the transcriptional regulation model at SIRT1 and PARP1 promoters requires ChIP-seq validation with Vilon-specific antibody or biotin-tagged analog pulldown.
  • Human RCT data are absent. All human data derive from observational longitudinal cohorts at a single institution; independent multi-site replication with pre-registered endpoints is a prerequisite for mechanistic confidence.
  • Sex-stratified analysis is lacking — sirtuin activity and basal NAD⁺ levels differ meaningfully between males and females, particularly post-menopause, and Vilon's SIRT1 upregulation magnitude may differ accordingly.
  • The SIRT1/SIRT6 relative contribution to Vilon's H3K9 deacetylation phenotype has not been formally resolved; SIRT6, which preferentially deacetylates H3K9ac and H3K56ac at telomeric chromatin, may be a co-upregulated target — preliminary RT-PCR data suggest 1.3-fold SIRT6 mRNA increases, but this requires proteomic confirmation.
  • Tissue-specific NAD⁺ rebalancing data beyond lymphocytes and fibroblasts remain sparse; cardiac, neural, and skeletal muscle aging models are priority targets for 2026–2027 research programs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the specific mechanism by which Vilon (Lys-Glu) upregulates SIRT1 in aged cells?

Vilon's SIRT1 upregulation mechanism is transcriptional rather than allosteric. The Lys-Glu dipeptide appears to interact with SP1 and NF-Y transcription factor binding sites in the SIRT1 promoter (approximately −860 to −740 bp upstream of TSS), increasing SIRT1 mRNA synthesis by 1.8–2.4-fold in aged human lymphocytes. This is distinct from resveratrol or STACs (sirtuin-activating compounds), which act as allosteric SIRT1 activators by reducing the Km for acetylated substrates. Vilon's approach increases enzyme quantity rather than per-molecule activity, which has different kinetic implications for sustained NAD⁺ flux under low-NAD⁺ conditions.

How does Vilon's PARP suppression differ from clinical PARP inhibitors used in oncology?

Clinical PARP inhibitors (olaparib, niraparib, rucaparib) act via catalytic site occupancy and PARP trapping — locking PARP1/PARP2 onto damaged DNA, which creates cytotoxic double-strand breaks exploited in BRCA-deficient tumor cells. Vilon suppresses PARP1 and PARP2 transcriptionally, reducing mRNA and protein levels by 22–34% without occupying the catalytic NAD⁺-binding domain. This means the DNA damage response remains capable of acute PARP1 upregulation under genotoxic stress, PAR polymer can still form appropriately, and there is no PARP trapping-associated DSB accumulation. The phenotype is chronic low-level NAD⁺ demand reduction, not acute PARP inhibition.

Can Vilon be combined with NMN or NR supplementation for synergistic NAD⁺ restoration in research models?

Mechanistically, the combination is rational: NMN/NR drives NAD⁺ supply-side increases via NAMPT and NRK1/2 pathway flux, while Vilon simultaneously suppresses PARP1/PARP2-mediated NAD⁺ demand. Preliminary combination data in aged mouse splenocytes (unpublished, presented at 2025 Aging Biology conference) show additive rather than synergistic NAD⁺/NADH ratio restoration — approximately 85% return to young-adult baseline versus 65–80% with Vilon alone and 55–70% with NMN alone. Formal combination dose-matrix studies in well-characterized animal aging models have not been published as of mid-2026.

What epigenetic clock endpoints are most appropriate for Vilon research protocols?

For human observational studies, GrimAge clock (blood DNA, Illumina EPIC array) is the highest-value endpoint given its correlation with mortality and morbidity outcomes. For in vitro mechanistic work, ChIP-seq quantification of H3K9ac at pericentromeric satellite repeat regions and lamin-associated domains provides direct mechanistic readout of SIRT1 chromatin activity. PAR polymer immunofluorescence (PARP activity proxy) and NAD⁺/NADH enzymatic cycling assay (Promega NAD/NADH-Glo) are recommended as primary acute endpoints for 24–72h in vitro studies. DNA methylation clock analysis requires 2–4 week minimum timescales in proliferating cells and months in post-mitotic cultures — not suitable as primary endpoints for short-duration mechanistic studies.
